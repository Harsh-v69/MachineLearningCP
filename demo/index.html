Solver outcome

Both graphs are built from the same two candidates (one optimal, one adversarial). The adversarial one never reaches the goal in either graph, so the solver picks the optimal path either way; validation and scoring do not change whether this episode succeeds. What they do change: the validated graph is smaller, since the dead branch is discarded rather than merely unused, and its solver cost reads much higher. That is not a bug: Phase 4 no longer prices an edge by confidence alone, it also charges for any step that moves a box farther from its goal and for how much of the planning round's step budget has already been spent, then scales everything up by 10x to keep one decimal of precision as a whole number for the solver. The chosen path stays identical either way; only the price tag attached to trusting it changes.

Does this matter at scale?

On the goal-biased mock LLM, corner mistakes like the one above almost never happen by chance, so the aggregate metrics look identical with or without the validator. To find out whether it actually matters, we stress-tested it with a deliberately much dumber candidate generator (pure random legal moves, no safety-net optimal plan) across 30 trials on a harder two-box level, then independently re-checked every rejection with a plain breadth-first search that has nothing to do with the validator's own logic.
