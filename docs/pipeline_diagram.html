Stage by stage

  1. Environment — any benchmark's step(state, action) and is_goal(state). Sokoban, river crossing, and Rush Hour are built and share every phase below unchanged; ALFWorld, MuSiQue, and GSM8K-Hard (TAPE's own named benchmarks) are the same abstraction, not yet implemented.
  2. Candidate plans (Phase 1) — the LLM proposes several action sequences from the current state, not one.
  3. Plan graph (Phase 1) — every candidate is simulated step by step; identical resulting states collapse onto one shared node.
  4. Validator (Phase 2) — each transition is checked against domain rules the environment itself doesn't enforce (e.g. a Sokoban corner deadlock). Rejected transitions never become edges; uncertain ones get a lower confidence instead of a hard reject.
  5. Experience store (Phase 3) — every executed transition's outcome (matched vs. mismatched prediction) is recorded per state/action, and multiplies that transition's confidence in future graphs.
  6. Cost (Phase 4) — combines confidence, how much a step regresses away from the goal, and how much of the step budget is spent, into one edge weight.
  7. Adaptive select (Phase 5) — A* (using the Phase 4 score as its heuristic) when the task is a single shortest-path problem; CP-SAT when multiple constraints have to be satisfied jointly.
  8. Validated optimal path — the lowest-cost route through a graph that has already had unrecoverable transitions removed and untrustworthy ones downweighted.
  9. Execute & replan — the path is run step by step; a mismatch between predicted and actual state triggers a fresh planning round from the real state, and both outcomes feed back into the experience store.
Phases 1–5 built and tested (54 automated tests) across three benchmarks (Sokoban, river crossing, Rush Hour) · Phase 6 (cross-benchmark evaluation) and TAPE's own remaining benchmarks (ALFWorld, MuSiQue, GSM8K-Hard) are what's left.
